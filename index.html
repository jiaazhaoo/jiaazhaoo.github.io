Email GitHub LinkedIn CV (PDF)

Two of the systems below decide questions with legal consequences: whether a property carries a planning condition that must be registered, and where on the national map an application's site actually sits. Both run on thirty- and forty-year-old scans that the character recogniser reads as This copy has beenmade by orwith the authorityofthe StrategicDirectorof. Neither is allowed to be confidently wrong — so both quote their evidence, measure their own confidence against hand-labelled truth, and hand the cases they cannot settle to a person.

The same habit shows up where there are no legal stakes at all. I deleted a lyric-transcription feature from a shipping macOS app after measuring it recognise exactly zero segments of sung vocals, and rejected a CoreML backend after measuring it 2.3× slower than plain CPU. I would rather delete a feature than ship one I cannot defend with a number.

Selected work

Reading the Paper Record

live demo · mirror

When you buy a house in England the council must disclose the legal conditions attached to it — that the garage may not become a shop, that the flats may not be occupied until the parking is built. Those conditions were written on paper decades ago and most have never been anything else. This workflow reads the scanned case file and answers one question per property: does it carry a condition that must be registered? Get it wrong one way and a buyer is not warned about a real restriction; get it wrong the other and a property is burdened with one that does not exist.

  • Nothing counts unless it is quoted, and every quote is then searched for in the recognised text. 22 of 23 verified against the scan; a verdict resting only on a rejected quote is not allowed to stand.
  • Two independent readers, never averaged. On one case the rules engine found no registrable condition and would have cleared the property. The LLM found three, quoted each, and every quote verified. The human reviewer had reached the same answer as the LLM.
  • Being unsure is a result, not a failure. Where two readers disagreed on a notice date, the field was marked single-sourced, its confidence dropped, and the discrepancy raised on the case's check list — rather than one being silently picked.
  • Confidence is computed, not asked for. Ask a model how confident it is and it says 90–95 whether right or wrong. The grade here is built from checkable facts: did two readers agree, was the wording visible on the page, was the condition read rather than inferred.
  • Verdict and decision date match the delivered, human-reviewed result on both demo cases.

Scan to Boundary

live demo

A UK district council holds thirty years of planning applications as scans and nobody knows where the sites are. This reads the scans, finds the plot the applicant drew in red, puts it on the national map in British National Grid, and grades its own answer — so a person only opens the cases it could not settle.

  • 12 real cases end to end: 57 pages read, 20 plans found, 19 traced, 9 matched to the map.
  • Tracing: 88% correct in the top confidence band across 97 blind pages, scored against boundaries drawn by hand. That band is the only one allowed to run without a person.
  • Placement: 8 of 8 accepted polygons were the right land. Zero false accepts. The one wrong placement was delivered as unverified — reported rather than asserted.
  • Five models from three vendors, one typed contract. Each answers through a schema and a malformed answer triggers a retry; three vote on where the plot is, and their disagreement feeds a confidence model fitted on 58 labelled pages and validated on 97 held out.
  • Checked against a system of record — addresses compared token by token with the official register, and the drawing's own scale and north arrow used to match the plot at real size and rotation before it counts.
  • A nightly regression fails the build if any case drops a confidence band or loses more than 0.05 of overlap against a committed baseline.

London Building Function

live map

Every building footprint in London with an inferred use, a height, and the evidence behind both — built entirely from free and open data, deliberately: no OS Places after the trial, no AddressBase, no Google Places, no commercial POI or footfall feed. The constraint is the point; it is what makes the result reproducible by anyone.

  • 95.64% of heights measured or surveyed from Environment Agency LiDAR, 0.05% modelled. Everything else is labelled as fallback rather than passed off as measured.
  • Use is stacked evidence, not a single source — OSM tags, OS Open UPRN counts, footprint area, LiDAR height, Food Standards Agency records, POI and retail-centre boundaries, energy certificates. Each join carries its own confidence, and a weaker join is never allowed to override a stronger one.
  • One café inside an office block stays a café. Tenant activity is kept separate from a building's primary use, so a single contained record cannot rewrite the whole building.
  • A per-building “personality” layer — dominant use, ground- and upper-floor signal, street context, and the evidence and caveats that produced them.

karanow

karanow.com · macOS app, shipped

Any audio playing on a Mac, turned into an instrumental in real time, with synced lyrics and a pitch track. A re-exported 1.5-second low-latency HTDemucs runs under a hard real-time budget: 1.125 seconds of audio must be separated every 1.125 seconds, or the backing track breaks. This is the project where I had to be right about performance, not hopeful.

  • 268 ms per window against a 1125 ms budget on an M2 Max — 24% used, four times the headroom. The app reports the figure to the user so they can see what their own machine is doing.
  • CoreML rejected after measuring it, not after assuming it: 2.3× slower than the CPU backend (622 ms vs 268 ms), because only 1072 of the graph's 1291 operators were supported and it fragmented into 98 partitions.
  • Idle CPU 40–60% → 1.5%, by noticing that a published property notifies whether or not the value actually changed. AppleScript on the main thread 55% → 0%, by moving player queries onto a serial queue.
  • 11.5 million songs of lyrics folded out of a 233 GiB dump into a 1.36 GB index plus 7.57 GB of packs the client fetches by byte range — because the index store caps at 10 GB and the text alone was 7.57 GB.
  • Shipped end to end, alone: Developer ID signing and Apple notarisation, OAuth through a Worker, Stripe billing in seven currencies, and Sparkle delta updates — a 509 KB patch against a 236 MB app, so a bug fix does not re-download the model.
  • Features deleted after measurement. Speech recognition transcribed test speech at 0.927 confidence and returned zero segments on ninety seconds of sung vocals, so that feature is gone. A per-feature lifetime unlock was withdrawn before anyone bought one, because “forever” is the wrong promise on a cost that recurs.

briffy

source

A desktop app that writes down what you saw, heard and were handed — screenshots, voice notes, clipboard, files — without asking you to file any of it. Recognition runs on your own machine: a picture is never sent to a model.

  • PP-OCRv6 and Whisper both run on-device through onnxruntime; search and retrieval work fully offline, with cloud models used only for titles and daily recaps.
  • Exact and semantic search over everything captured, with speaker separation on voice notes and automatic daily summaries.
  • Source-available under PolyForm Noncommercial; four interchangeable model providers so no single vendor is load-bearing.

How I work

  • A number, or it did not happen. Every claim on this page comes from a run I can reproduce — held-out pages, blind labels, benchmarks, a committed baseline a nightly job checks against.
  • Uncertainty is an output, not an embarrassment. A workflow that says “I could not settle this” is worth more than one that is right slightly more often and gives you no way to tell which answers are the wrong ones.
  • Models propose; deterministic code decides. Typed schemas at every boundary, quotes checked back against the source, and a fixed decision order — so the answer can be explained to somebody who does not trust language models at all.
  • Deleting is a result. Three features in the work above were removed after measurement. Each deletion is recorded with the measurement that justified it, so nobody rediscovers the same dead end.

Design. Not as decoration — as the part of the work where you find out whether anyone can actually use it. In karanow that meant exactly one saturated element on screen at a time, contrast checked against WCAG across all six theme-by-appearance combinations, and a rule written into a test so a layout relationship cannot quietly break. When everybody misread a horizontal slider labelled “vocal removal” as a volume control, the fix was not a better label: it stopped being a slider and became six stepped blocks. A control that is read wrong is a control that is built wrong.
