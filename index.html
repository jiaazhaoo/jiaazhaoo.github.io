Email GitHub

I work on UK property and planning geography: taking a scanned decision notice or a single line of free-text address and resolving it all the way down to a coordinate, a parcel polygon, and the planning history attached to it. The hard part is never the happy path — it is the 20% where the OCR is smudged, the address matches three towns, the survey point lands in a shared yard instead of the building, and the upstream dataset is simply wrong.

I build the boring infrastructure that survives that: measured thresholds instead of guessed ones, stable module contracts, documented failure modes, and honest confidence scores that say “low” when the evidence is thin. LLMs get used where they earn their place — and deliberately removed where a rule and a threshold do the job more cheaply and more predictably.

What I build

  • Address → coordinate → parcel — gazetteer matching, confidence scoring, polygon derivation.
  • Document pipelines — scanned records through OCR into structured, geocoded rows.
  • Open-data fusion — OSM, LiDAR, UPRN, EPC, footfall joined into one evidence layer.
  • Spatial ML — trained merge, ranking and reranking models over geometry features.
  • WebGIS front ends — vector tiles and interactive maps over half a million features.
  • Shipped products — from data pipeline to App Store listing, end to end.

Address → parcel → planning history, as one web service

Type one line of free-text UK address; get back a derived parcel polygon on an EPSG:27700-native map, plus only the planning applications that actually fall inside it. Filtering by postcode alone returns everything on the street — the spatial filter is what makes the answer useful.

Two fixes mattered most. A search-scope reasoner that walks the address backwards to pin the right town lifted one industrial-estate address from a match 600 km away in Scotland to the correct unit. And a survey-point-quality check caught cases where the official point lands in a shared yard, snapping the result off a 1.8 m sliver and onto the actual building.

Planning noise 315 → 2 · sliver 149.6 m² (0.06 compactness) → building 261.3 m² (0.73) · 35 tests green, zero regressions

FastAPI · Leaflet + Proj4 · OS Places / Features WFS / Maps WMTS · EPSG:27700

Scanned paper records → geocoded parcels, in production

A production line for statutory land and planning records held by UK local authorities: raw scan preprocessing, document classification, OCR, extraction of the site address and decision date, geocoding to a national grid coordinate, then derivation of the parcel polygon from Ordnance Survey topography.

Built council-agnostic on purpose. Area-specific resources resolve through a profile object, so onboarding a new authority means adding configuration, not branching the algorithms — enforced by a written module boundary with stable input and output contracts.

One address in → zero or one polygon out · new council = config, not code

Python · geopandas · scikit-learn · OCR · OS MasterMap WFS

London Street Personality Map

A map-first read of what every street in Greater London is actually for — daily consumption, nightlife, office, transit gateway, industrial, regeneration — inferred from buildings, POIs, transport and footfall, then explained back to the user with its own evidence, confidence and risk notes.

Built entirely on free and open data. Paid sources are excluded by design, which forced the interesting work: filling building-height gaps from Environment Agency LiDAR, and inferring residential subtypes conservatively from UPRN density, footprint area and height rather than trusting a single tag.

572,891 street segments · 12 street types · 95.6% measured building heights, 128 missing

TypeScript · React · MapLibre GL · tippecanoe · OSM / LiDAR / UPRN / EPC / TfL

Airbnb host commercialization, mapped

Where does short-let commercialization actually concentrate, and how has it moved? A reusable collector pulls Inside Airbnb data for any city into PostgreSQL — fuzzy city matching, resumable downloads, host-level aggregation — and an interactive map classifies hosts into five commercialization tiers and animates them over time.

Live at geonarvis.com · packaged collector, not a notebook · grew out of my MSc dissertation
