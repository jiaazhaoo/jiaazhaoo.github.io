Two of the systems below decide questions with legal consequences: whether a property carries a planning condition that must be registered, and where on the national map an application's site actually sits.

Both run on thirty- and forty-year-old scans that the character recogniser reads as This copy has beenmade by orwith the authorityofthe StrategicDirectorof. Neither is allowed to be confidently wrong — so both quote their evidence, measure their own confidence against hand-labelled truth, and hand the cases they cannot settle to a person.

The same habit shows up where there are no legal stakes at all. I deleted a lyric-transcription feature from a shipping macOS app after measuring it recognise exactly zero segments of sung vocals, and rejected a CoreML backend after measuring it 2.3× slower than plain CPU. I would rather delete a feature than ship one I cannot defend with a number.

01

Reading the Paper Record

Planning decisions → the legal register

Live demo ↗

When you buy a house in England the council must disclose the legal conditions attached to it — that the garage may not become a shop, that the flats may not be occupied until the parking is built. Those conditions were written on paper decades ago. This reads the scanned case file and answers one question per property: does it carry a condition that must be registered?

22/23quotes verified back against the scan
2independent readers, never averaged
  • Nothing counts unless it is quoted. The model is asked for the answer and the exact words it read it from; every quote is then searched for in the recognised text. A verdict resting only on a rejected quote cannot stand.
  • Disagreement is never averaged away. On one case the rules engine found no registrable condition and would have cleared the property. The LLM found three, quoted each, and all verified — matching what the human reviewer had concluded.
  • Confidence is computed, not asked for. Ask a model how confident it is and it says 90–95 whether right or wrong. This grade is built from checkable facts: did two readers agree, was the wording visible, was the condition read rather than inferred.

02

Scan to Boundary

Thirty years of scans → polygons on the national map

Live demo ↗

A UK district council holds thirty years of planning applications as scans and nobody knows where the sites are. This reads the scans, finds the plot the applicant drew in red, puts it on the national map in British National Grid, and grades its own answer — so a person only opens the cases it could not settle.

88%top-band trace accuracy, 97 blind pages
0false accepts on placement
5models, 3 vendors, one typed contract
  • The uncertain case is reported, not asserted. Eight of eight accepted polygons were the right land; the one wrong placement was delivered as unverified with every rejected candidate drawn for the reviewer.
  • Three models vote, and their disagreement is a feature. It feeds a confidence model fitted on 58 labelled pages and validated on 97 held out. The top band is the only one allowed to run without a person.
  • A nightly regression fails the build if any case drops a confidence band or loses more than 0.05 of overlap against a committed baseline.

03

London Building Function

Every footprint in London, with its evidence

Live map ↗

Every building footprint in London with an inferred use, a height, and the evidence behind both — built entirely from free and open data, deliberately: no OS Places after the trial, no AddressBase, no Google Places, no commercial POI feed. The constraint is the point; it is what makes the result reproducible by anyone.

95.64%heights measured or surveyed from LiDAR
0.05%modelled — the rest labelled as fallback
  • Use is stacked evidence, not one source. OSM tags, UPRN counts, footprint area, LiDAR height, Food Standards Agency records, POI and retail-centre boundaries, energy certificates — each join carries its own confidence, and a weaker join is never allowed to override a stronger one.
  • One café inside an office block stays a café. Tenant activity is kept separate from a building's primary use, so a single contained record cannot rewrite the whole building.

04

karanow

Real-time AI karaoke for macOS — shipped, alone

karanow.com ↗

Any audio playing on a Mac, turned into an instrumental in real time, with synced lyrics and a pitch track. A re-exported 1.5-second HTDemucs runs under a hard real-time budget: 1.125 seconds of audio must be separated every 1.125 seconds, or the backing track breaks. This is the project where I had to be right about performance, not hopeful.

24%of the real-time budget used — 4× headroom
40% → 1.5%idle CPU, after one root cause
509 KBupdate patch against a 236 MB app
  • CoreML rejected after measuring it, not after assuming it: 2.3× slower than the CPU backend, because only 1072 of the graph's 1291 operators were supported and it fragmented into 98 partitions.
  • 11.5 million songs of lyrics folded out of a 233 GiB dump into a 1.36 GB index plus packs the client fetches by byte range — because the index store caps at 10 GB and the text alone was 7.57 GB.
  • Shipped end to end: Developer ID signing and Apple notarisation, OAuth through a Worker, Stripe billing in seven currencies, and Sparkle delta updates so a bug fix does not re-download the model.

How I work

A number, or it did not happen.

Every claim on this page comes from a run I can reproduce — held-out pages, blind labels, benchmarks, a committed baseline a nightly job checks against.

Uncertainty is an output.

A workflow that says “I could not settle this” is worth more than one that is right slightly more often and gives you no way to tell which answers are the wrong ones.

Models propose; code decides.

Typed schemas at every boundary, quotes checked back against the source, a fixed decision order — so the answer can be explained to somebody who does not trust language models at all.

Deleting is a result.

Three features in the work above were removed after measurement. Each deletion is recorded with the measurement that justified it, so nobody rediscovers the same dead end.

Design

Not as decoration — as the part where you find out whether anyone can use it. In karanow that meant exactly one saturated element on screen at a time, contrast checked against WCAG across all six theme-by-appearance combinations, and a layout rule written into a test so it cannot quietly break. When everybody misread a slider labelled “vocal removal” as a volume control, the fix was not a better label: it stopped being a slider.
